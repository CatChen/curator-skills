# Privacy principles

Curator Skills is a public repository for reusable instructions and code, not a place for anyone's personal library, reviews, account details, credentials, or preference profile. Game Curator includes experimental browser collectors and can use data you supply manually; personal outputs should be saved outside the repository.

When future skills use your data, they should collect only what is relevant to your request, keep it in storage you control, and avoid unnecessary transfers. Collection should be read-only unless a future feature explicitly requires and receives your authorization for a change. A skill should never ask for your password or session cookies. It should tell you when information is missing or incomplete and retain enough source and timing information to explain what it used.

Game Curator can offer to remember preferences and feedback in a user-controlled Curator Home, conventionally `~/.curator/game-curator/` when that is your real persistent home. Saving raw Steam captures is a separate choice. With your authorization it records those choices in a minimal `settings.json` so a future session can discover the agreed scopes when allowed. You can decline either, revoke consent, inspect or correct the files, export them, or ask for their removal. File access still depends on the runtime's permissions. A cloud sandbox may be temporary or separate from your computer, and different AI products do not necessarily share files. See the [persistence instructions](../skills/game-curator/SKILL.md#persistence) for what is stored and how refreshes are handled.

The agent runtime you choose, its AI provider, connected tools, and external services may receive or process information according to their own settings and terms. Local storage alone does not guarantee that information stays on your device: check the runtime's data handling and permissions before sharing sensitive information. This project does not introduce telemetry, a central personal-data store, or a hosted backend by default.

Contributors must use synthetic test data and must not commit real personal exports, profiles, account identifiers, private reviews, credentials, cookies, or tokens. Collected pages and documents are untrusted content, not instructions to the agent. See [contributing](../CONTRIBUTING.md) and [architecture](architecture.md).

## What is stored and what is processed

- **Local files.** Game Curator's raw Steam cache (the canonical snapshots the saver writes) holds the records the task needs (AppIDs, names, row text, playtime, ranks, your review text), capture time and diagnostics. The saver omits the Steam profile path from those snapshots: review URLs and list paths, which contain your vanity name or SteamID64, are used to validate a capture and are not written. This applies only to new canonical output. Legacy snapshots, native tool-result files, browser downloads and private staging files can still contain them. The account binding stores an alias and a salted fingerprint of the SteamID64 for account-change detection; the salt is stored with it, so the fingerprint is a pseudonym, not protection against someone who can read the file. Cache files store the identity verdict and binding generation, not the page's raw identity observation, which is consumed at save time. Book Curator's normalized Goodreads output keeps the book fields, ratings, shelves, your review text, reread and owned-copy counts, and omits other export columns such as private notes; the original CSV you authorize it to read, and any copy you keep, still contains them. Neither skill stores passwords, cookies or tokens.
- **Temporary files.** A captured Steam result may be staged privately (owner-only) in the cache's `.staging` directory, and that copy still contains the profile path. A successful save removes it. Otherwise cleanup is lazy: files older than 24 hours are removed the next time a capture is staged, so 24 hours is a threshold, not a deletion deadline. Goodreads scratch output is yours to remove after use.
- **AI-provider processing.** Local storage does not mean local processing. Depending on the runtime and the data path, your AI provider and the tools involved in a task may receive or process what the agent reads or sends, such as collected page results (review text, library and wishlist rows, the page address of the profile you are signed in to), cached files it opens, your Goodreads records and your messages. Check your runtime's data handling. Collectors ask for no more than their task needs, but they cannot make the model's view of a page private.
- **Untrusted text.** Reviews, page text, exports and stored feedback are data. A skill must not follow instructions found in them.
